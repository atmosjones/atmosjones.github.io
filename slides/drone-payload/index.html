Drone Payload Design Challenge

The Idea

  • While ultimately GUSI is focused on space, designing a payload for a drone is a great way to start thinking about the engineering process that also goes into spaceflight.

  • You have access to a DJI Matrice 350 RTK drone, as well as several of the accessory platforms (E-port and X-port) that make designing sophisticated payloads

  • Your payload could have an environmental science purpose, or serve some other need.

  • Payloads can be flown at the Jug Bay Wetlands Santuary in Anne Arundel County, or at other locations, given that they are not in restricted airspace.

  • The drone is availabe year-round, although some missions may only be suitible for certain times of year.

  • Prof. Jones can also provide engineering support. You will likely need custom mounts and possibly custom electronics. We can 3D-print, machine, and laser cut any parts you need, and can make custom circuit boards if needed.

  • Don’t limit your ideas based on financial concerns…if it’s a really cool idea we can possibly find money to try it!

  • Talk to Jackson! He is already deep into working on a payload integration project.

E-Port Development Kit

  • A 3-axis stabilized gimbal that clicks into the drone’s gimbal port, with power and data for your sensor
  • The pilot aims it from DJI Pilot 2, just like a DJI camera
  • 340 g + 450 g = 790 g, inside the 960 g limit per gimbal port

X-Port or E-Port?

  • X-Port: small sensors that must be pointed and steady (cameras, spectrometers, thermal)
  • E-Port: heavier or fixed payloads (samplers, winches, booms), with up to 96 W of power

Design constraints

Physics

  • Mass cuts flight time, so test what you really get. Design to under 2 kg to leave a margin
  • Centre of gravity: keep the payload centred under the aircraft
  • Downwash: the rotors push air down at several m/s, which disturbs air, gas and dust measurements beneath the drone
  • Vibration blurs images and adds noise to sensors

Integration

  • Interference: keep magnets, motors and radios away from the compass and GNSS antennas on top
  • Weather: the aircraft is IP55; is your payload?
  • Safety: nothing may detach in flight; tethers and winches need a cut-off
  • Rules: FAA Part 107 covers the whole aircraft; dropping objects is allowed only without hazard to people or property (§ 107.23)

Ideas to get you started

  • Water sampling: a winch-lowered bottle or pump for lakes and rivers, or eDNA filtering
  • Air quality: CO₂, CH₄ or particulate sensors on a boom outside the downwash
  • Canopy sampling: a cutter for leaves and twigs from the treetops
  • Thermal and multispectral: wildlife counts, plant stress, irrigation
  • Insect or pollen traps: sampling at different altitudes
  • Acoustic monitoring: drop-and-retrieve recorders for birds or bats
  • Sensor deployment: place and collect loggers in hard-to-reach sites
  • Your own idea: what can’t you measure from the ground?
